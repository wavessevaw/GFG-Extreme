# Experimental Governor floor check — rejected

**Classification:** deterministic software model. **Decision:** rejected; not an implementation or device result.

The historical stress case reports prolonged x3.75 when a simulated 34 fps game can hold x3 below 15 W. In a recorded trace, the Governor first tests 13 W, restores 15 W and declares that level stable because the failed floor lies within 2.5 W. The pilot then demotes the point after sustained high TDP. This is a model observation and does not prove the same cause on a Deck.

The hypothesis was to defer a stable lock **only** in Autopilot above 14.5 W when the last failed level is more than 1.5 W below the current limit, allowing another smaller step. It was injected at runtime into `_stable_check`; product files were not changed. Before execution, the screening rule in the experiment script required rejection if any case gained seconds below 93% of its current point, had more TDP writes, or consumed more simulated APU energy over the same session. This strict screen does not define a release threshold or account for measurement noise on hardware.

The experiment used ten cases: `fps15` 22, 26, 30, 34, 40 crossed with SteamOS limits 15 and 18 W, each with seed 1, 1,200 virtual seconds, Autopilot, a 70% battery, 30 °C ambient and above-x3 enabled. The 34 fps cases reduced the last-800-second x3.75 count from 313 to 0 and below-93%-of-point seconds from 23 to 1, but simulated session energy increased from approximately 3.886 to 4.498 Wh. In the 40 fps cases, TDP writes increased from 2 to 5 at a 15 W limit and from 3 to 6 at an 18 W limit. Thus the predeclared screen fails. Modelled energy and temperature are not hardware measurements, and the different operating points make this neither a controlled image-quality comparison nor an estimate of real energy consumption.

The result covers only these scenarios, and the `real_below_93pct_seconds` field counts seconds relative to the current requested point, including transitions.

Next work should investigate whether the Governor can distinguish a genuinely expensive point from a temporary high limit without adding unproved probes or suppressing the earlier anti-oscillation fixes. Any proposal must be compared independently against 3.2.2, current accepted stable, and the candidate, with predeclared frame pacing, energy and controller-transition measures; evidence on Steam Deck remains UNVERIFIED.
