# GFG Extreme — Known Limitations

Items are kept in the order they were found; each says what is known as of the current 1.0.x release.

1. **Limited hardware coverage.** Validated in real play on a Steam Deck. Coverage of Deck models, docked play and games is still limited. Everything is also covered by unit, replay, service and real-bash wrapper tests; the replay corpus now includes lines captured on a Deck.
2. **`fixed-plan` cadence.** Confirmed on a Deck: `fixed-plan` events arrive about every 1–2 s while frame generation is active, which is enough for the Governor's windows; during loading screens the renderer emits no FPS at all (shown as "waiting for FPS").
3. **Native (x1) points may have no evidence.** With Frame Generation off the Renderer may emit no periodic FPS event, so a native trial could time out and be rejected.
4. **Restart-bound toggles.** Since v0.0.8 every managed launch carries renderer diagnostics, and the leased standby overlay, so the Governor turns on in a running game; once the Governor or the HUD has been used, launches also load a hidden HUD, so it toggles live too. Still process-static: Frame Generation provisioning (a profile with FG not provisioned at launch cannot get it live), the Scaling Engine (scaled points need the opt-in *Scale-ready launch* and a relaunch), and anything about a game started before v0.0.8, without the launch command, or while the plugin was not running.
5. **Saved snapshot while running.** After the Governor is disabled, a running game keeps the Saved snapshot taken at launch; later Saved edits apply on the next launch.
6. **Guard.** Battery and Balanced react to heavier scenes all session (fast watts, deeper ratio, then more watts); Quality re-checks its point at the ceiling and moves to a cheaper one when it cannot hold.
7. **Dock.** Docked play uses a 60 FPS target; interaction with the Automatic Dock state machine has had little real-world testing.
8. **Overlay directory in Flatpak.** The overlay lives next to the Saved config and is assumed readable from Flatpak sandboxes; unverified.
9. **In-game overlay.** MangoHud horizontal layout and the refresh rate of the `exec` status line are unverified on device. With shader effects (vkBasalt) on, the overlay is stacked after vkBasalt; it is skipped only when the profile loads its own MangoHud, and inside Flatpak launches.
10. **TDP control** goes through `steamos-manager` (Steam's own TDP path, `steamosctl` / D-Bus `TdpLimit1`), whole watts, read back from the hwmon caps. Without root only that path exists, inside Steam's TDP range. With root, values outside Steam's range (above the usual 15 W, where the device allows it) and the exact restore of the original fast/slow caps use direct hwmon writes. Through the manager fast PPT equals slow PPT. If the TDP limit is switched on in the Steam quick menu, Steam re-applies its own value on game or focus changes and after sleep; the Governor treats that as an outside change and hands control back after a few attempts. Watts above Steam's range are written to hwmon directly, so Steam's own TdpLimit keeps its old value and any re-apply by Steam resets them (seen as an outside change). TDP display needs readable hwmon values (otherwise `TDPn/a`).
11. **GFG Effort thresholds** (nightmare below 18 real FPS, dwell times) are design choices not yet tuned on real games.
12. **UI.** Frontend is verified in a mock Decky environment (Chromium) with sample data; focus/controller navigation on a real Deck is untested. Shader-effect and per-field presets from the old UI are reachable only through *All settings*.
13. **External backends** (OptiScaler, Game Native) are observe-only; the Governor never changes them.
14. **Fractional multipliers (x1.25 to x2.75) are unverified on hardware.** It relies on switching the renderer between fixed and adaptive mode live and on `adaptive-plan` interval telemetry. A renderer that does not reach the 1.5 ratio fails the confirmation, times out and rolls back, so the worst case is losing that rung, not a wrong setting. A full unsuccessful ladder is up to 12 attempts of 25 s to 60 s each, so finding the right point on a very demanding game can take several minutes.
15. **Battery mode thresholds.** TDP steps are judged on FPS evidence and the measured APU draw. The budget thresholds (9–11 / 15 W and the device maximum, real floor 24, emergency after 60 s short of the cap) are design choices and may need tuning for other games.
16. **Battery mode raises TDP above the QAM value** (up to 15 W, and above that only as a last resort), but never above the hardware maximum read from `powerN_cap_max`: a stock Steam Deck OLED stops at 15 W, so the last-resort tier does not exist there and the guard ends at x4. The original caps are restored on stop, unload, profile switch or a new game session.
17. **Battery mode keeps probing lower watts (v0.0.9).** While the game holds, it tries 1 W less every 45 s; a probe that fails backs off to 90, 180 and 300 s. A failed probe is reverted by the per-second check, so it shows as a dip of about 2–3 s, not a whole window. Raising is fast (about 2 s) only when the measured draw sits at the cap, and only up to 11 W; 12–15 W and above still go through the slow path, which deepens the multiplier first. Without a draw sensor there is no fast path and lower watts are tried every 5 min. A level that just failed is not retried at once (item 28).
18. **Loading screens vs. a starved game.** Real FPS below half the cap with the measured APU draw far under the cap is treated as a loading screen or a menu and never buys watts quickly. With the draw at the cap it is a starved game (for example right after leaving a pause menu that walked TDP down) and gets the working level back at once. A CPU-heavy loading screen that pins the cap does get watts; Battery mode lowers them again afterwards.
19. **Host sensors.** Temperature, GPU busy, per-core CPU load, fan, battery draw and the bottleneck verdict come from sysfs/procfs. A missing sensor shows as "–" and the verdict falls back to "unknown". Decisions that use them: no render-scale points for a CPU-bound game, and no quality probes while the APU is hot (item 27).
20. **Interface.** The navigation was reorganised in v0.0.10 (Home → Details / Settings) following the UI audit; it has been checked only in the mock Decky environment, not with a controller on a real Deck.
21. **Game memory (v0.0.11).** After a point has held for 90 s the Governor stores point and TDP under (profile, target, mode) in `gfg-game-models.json`; the next session starts locked on it instead of searching. Since 1.0.2 the key is the game's Steam AppID when the launch recorded one (otherwise the profile name). Points that failed to hold are remembered for 10 minutes from the failure (a reload keeps the original expiry), so a mode switch or plugin reload does not repeat a probe that just failed; after that a lighter scene may try it again. A remembered state that no longer holds is corrected by the normal guard (a short dip, not a stuck setting). Entries expire after 30 days. Not yet tested on a Deck.
22. **Balanced mode (v0.0.12).** Same engine as Battery with a higher floor: it starts at about 45 real FPS and 12 W, offers no point below 30 real FPS, never uses the last-resort ratio and never goes above the Deck's normal power range. The numbers (45/12/13 W/30) are design choices not yet tuned on real games. Game memory is kept separately per mode.
23. **Log verdict (v0.0.13).** `summary.txt` in every recorded log, and the "What the log shows" card after Stop, state what the log contains (missing diagnostics, no FPS samples, long PAUSED, failed self-test checks, rejected points, target vs. output). It does not explain why; the thresholds (90% of target, 50% telemetry availability, 30% PAUSED, 85 °C) are rules of thumb. `tools/gfg_log_report.py <zip>` prints the same report.
24. **Overlay changes while playing (v0.0.17).** MangoHud re-reads its config on every change; rapid changes while playing may be unsafe. Rewrites of the live config are limited to one per 5 s (the newest state wins).
25. **Fractional points under a power limit (v0.0.17).** When a fractional point is requested but the GPU cannot feed its real-frame cap, the adaptive renderer may deliver the full target with a deeper ratio instead. The Governor now takes that as the deeper point holding and moves there, instead of rejecting the request.
26. **Generated-frame capacity (v0.0.19).** The renderer reports how many generated frames per real frame it has resources for (`generated_frame_capacity`, 2 = up to x3). Raising it needs a swapchain recreation, so requests beyond it fall back to a lower fixed ratio. The Governor now offers only ratios the *current* capacity allows (capacity + 1; 0 means native only). It is re-read every step, so a later report after a swapchain recreation lifts the ceiling again; until the renderer has reported a capacity, the old behaviour applies. A fractional point that the GPU cannot feed (33x2.75 or 36x2.5 at 10–13 W) is recognised after about 8 s of consistent evidence (since 1.0.1; it used to wait for the 25 s timeout) and the delivered point is used; output stays at the target meanwhile.
27. **Heat (1.0.4).** When the host sensors call the APU *hot* (less than 8 °C below 90 °C) or *heating* (above 70 °C and rising faster than 1.5 °C/min), Battery and Balanced do not try fewer generated frames, because more real frames mean more watts and more heat. Lower-watt probes and the guard continue as before. The skipped step is tried once the APU has read *ok* for 90 s (since 1.0.5; the heating verdict flips around its thresholds). Quality mode is unchanged. The thresholds are rules of thumb from the host sensors, not tuned on a Deck; without a temperature sensor nothing changes.
28. **Failed lower-power probes (1.0.5).** A TDP level that just failed with the current point is not probed again for 2 minutes, then 4, 8 and at most 10 minutes if it keeps failing, even after a guard raise and a successful walk back down. The block is lifted early when the game draws clearly less than that level (a lighter scene) or when the level holds. In a lighter scene without a draw sensor the lower level can therefore be found up to 10 minutes later than before.
29. **Quality mode failures (1.0.6).** Quality now rejects a request in about 8 s when the renderer applied it but holds the target with a deeper ratio (it used to wait for the 25 s timeout). Points that did not hold in Quality are remembered for 10 minutes per game, like Battery, so switching modes and back does not repeat them. Quality has no game memory of the point that held; every session still searches from the highest quality down.
